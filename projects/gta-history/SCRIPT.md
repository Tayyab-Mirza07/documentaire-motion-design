# GTA: the bug that built an empire

Script package for an 8-minute historical documentary, built for the `documentaire-motion-design` skill.

- **Scenes:** 28 · **Spoken words:** 1054 · **Estimated runtime:** 8:01 (engine estimate: 2.4 words/s + 1.5 s per scene; the real runtime follows the voice tracks)
- **Narration file:** [`src/gta/narration.json`](src/gta/narration.json), same text as below, ready for `creerFilm`
- **Suggested style:** `nuit` (dark background, glowing shapes), with a pink/cyan accent from scene 13 on
- **Visual rule:** everything is drawn as original shapes. No Rockstar logos, box art, screenshots or game footage.

## Title

**The Bug That Created GTA: The Full Story (1997–2026)**

Alternatives:

1. How a Glitch Turned Into GTA: 29 Years in 8 Minutes
2. GTA Was Supposed to Be a Different Game. Then It Broke.
3. The History of GTA: From a Failing Racing Game to GTA 6

Thumbnail idea: a top-down police car ramming a small red car, the word **BUG** crossed out and replaced by **GTA**, plus one number: `480M`.

## Hook (first 30 seconds, scenes 1 to 3)

> The mid nineteen-nineties. In Dundee, Scotland, a small studio is building a game about cops chasing robbers through a city. The testers are bored. The project is in trouble, and close to being cancelled.
>
> Then a bug appears. The police cars turn aggressive. They stop chasing the player's car and start ramming it off the road. It is pure chaos. And suddenly, the testers cannot stop playing.
>
> That accident became Grand Theft Auto. Nearly four hundred and eighty million copies sold. Debates in parliament. Court cases. Some of the biggest launches entertainment has ever seen. This is how a glitch became a giant.

Why it works: it opens on a concrete moment (a dull game about to be cancelled), turns on one surprise (the bug), and makes a promise the film pays off in the last scene.

## Scene-by-scene script

### The accident

#### 1. A failing game · `01-hook` · 0:00

**Voice-over (34 words):** The mid nineteen-nineties. In Dundee, Scotland, a small studio is building a game about cops chasing robbers through a city. The testers are bored. The project is in trouble, and close to being cancelled.

**Visual:** Night map of Scotland, a pin drops on Dundee. A tiny top-down city grid; one blue car follows one red car, slowly. A 'FUN' meter sits near zero, then a 'CANCELLED?' stamp hovers.

**On-screen text:** DUNDEE, SCOTLAND · mid-1990s · **Source:** A

#### 2. The bug · `02-bug` · 0:15

**Voice-over (33 words):** Then a bug appears. The police cars turn aggressive. They stop chasing the player's car and start ramming it off the road. It is pure chaos. And suddenly, the testers cannot stop playing.

**Visual:** Screen glitch. Police cars multiply and slam into the player car, sparks on each hit (sync on 'ramming'). The FUN meter shoots to the top on 'cannot stop playing'.

**On-screen text:** BUG → FEATURE · **Source:** A

#### 3. The promise · `03-promise` · 0:30

**Voice-over (36 words):** That accident became Grand Theft Auto. Nearly four hundred and eighty million copies sold. Debates in parliament. Court cases. Some of the biggest launches entertainment has ever seen. This is how a glitch became a giant.

**Visual:** Title card. Counter runs up to ~480M; three icons pop in on their words: parliament building, judge's gavel, stack of cash. Title lands last.

**On-screen text:** Title + '≈480 million copies' · **Source:** B

### 1997: Race'n'Chase

#### 4. DMA Design · `04-dundee` · 0:47

**Voice-over (36 words):** The studio was called DMA Design. It was founded by David Jones, and it was already famous for the puzzle game Lemmings. Its new project had a working title: Race'n'Chase. A living city, seen from above.

**Visual:** Studio building with a 'DMA DESIGN' sign. A row of small walking figures (a nod to Lemmings, original shapes). A design document slides in titled 'Race'n'Chase'.

**On-screen text:** DMA DESIGN · Race'n'Chase · **Source:** A

#### 5. Cop or criminal · `05-choice` · 1:03

**Voice-over (38 words):** Players could be the cop or the criminal. Almost nobody chose the cop. Obeying the rules was slow. Breaking them was fun. So the team followed the fun, and gave the game a new name: Grand Theft Auto.

**Visual:** Split screen: COP on the left, CRIMINAL on the right. Player figures slide one by one to the right side. 'Race'n'Chase' is crossed out and replaced by 'Grand Theft Auto'.

**On-screen text:** COP vs CRIMINAL · **Source:** A

#### 6. Release · `06-1997` · 1:21

**Voice-over (36 words):** It was released in late 1997, on PC and then PlayStation. Tiny cars, a bird's-eye view, and three cities to cause trouble in: Liberty City, Vice City and San Andreas. Remember those names. They come back.

**Visual:** Timeline marker '1997'. Three top-down city tiles pop in on their names, each with its own colour. A small 'to be continued' arrow on each.

**On-screen text:** LIBERTY CITY · VICE CITY · SAN ANDREAS · **Source:** C

#### 7. Manufactured outrage · `07-scandal` · 1:37

**Voice-over (40 words):** Before the release, the publisher hired a tabloid publicist, Max Clifford. His plan was to stir up outrage, on purpose. It worked. In May 1997, months before anyone could buy it, the game was raised in the House of Lords.

**Visual:** Newspaper front pages spin in with angry headlines (generic, invented mastheads). Cut to a parliament silhouette with a speech bubble containing a small car.

**On-screen text:** HOUSE OF LORDS · 20 May 1997 · **Source:** D

#### 8. The formula · `08-lesson` · 1:56

**Voice-over (32 words):** Every angry headline was free advertising. British censors gave the game an eighteen certificate, and it sold anyway. The series had found its formula: total freedom, dark satire, and controversy as fuel.

**Visual:** An '18' badge stamps onto the box. Three cards flip up on their words: FREEDOM, SATIRE, CONTROVERSY. A sales line climbs behind them.

**On-screen text:** 18 · FREEDOM · SATIRE · CONTROVERSY · **Source:** D

### 1998: Rockstar

#### 9. The Housers · `09-rockstar` · 2:10

**Voice-over (40 words):** Two British brothers were watching closely: Sam and Dan Houser. In 1998, in New York, they helped found a new label inside the publisher Take-Two. They called it Rockstar Games. A sequel followed in 1999. The real leap came next.

**Visual:** Plane arc from London to New York over a dark map. Two figures on a skyline. A star-shaped badge (original, not the real logo) pops on 'Rockstar Games'. Small '1999 · GTA 2' tick on the timeline.

**On-screen text:** NEW YORK · 1998 · **Source:** E*

### 2001: The third dimension

#### 10. GTA III · `10-gta3` · 2:29

**Voice-over (38 words):** October 2001. Grand Theft Auto III brings the camera down to street level, on PlayStation 2. For the first time, Liberty City is a full three-dimensional world. You can walk it, drive it, and get lost in it.

**Visual:** The flat top-down city tilts and extrudes into 3D blocks (isometric SVG). Camera 'drops' behind a car. Timeline marker '2001'.

**On-screen text:** 2D → 3D · October 2001 · **Source:** F

#### 11. After September 11 · `11-sept11` · 2:46

**Voice-over (49 words):** The timing was brutal. The game was set in a version of New York, and it was due weeks after September 11. Rockstar delayed it, cut a few pieces of content, and redesigned the cover. The changes were small: about one percent of the game, by Rockstar's own estimate.

**Visual:** Calendar pages: September → October, the launch pin sliding later. A box cover outline is swapped for a new panel-grid layout (abstract). Sober treatment, no imagery of the attacks.

**On-screen text:** Delayed · edited · new cover · **Source:** F

#### 12. The open world · `12-openworld` · 3:08

**Voice-over (43 words):** The idea was simple. Here is a city. Do what you want. Follow the story, or ignore it. Steal a taxi and pick up fares. Rival studios took notes, and within a few years the open world was a genre of its own.

**Visual:** A city map with one straight 'STORY' line, then dozens of branching paths lighting up. A taxi picks up a figure on 'taxi'. Other studio buildings light up around the map.

**On-screen text:** DO WHAT YOU WANT · **Source:** F

### 2002–2004: Pop culture

#### 13. Vice City · `13-vicecity` · 3:27

**Voice-over (45 words):** One year later came Vice City. A neon postcard of nineteen-eighties Miami: pastel suits, palm trees, a radio full of real hits from the decade, and Hollywood actors in the cast. Grand Theft Auto was not only a game any more. It was pop culture.

**Visual:** Palette shifts to pink/cyan. Palm trees, sunset disc, a car radio with bouncing equaliser bars (sync on 'radio'). Timeline marker '2002'.

**On-screen text:** VICE CITY · 2002 · **Source:** G*

#### 14. San Andreas · `14-sanandreas` · 3:48

**Voice-over (42 words):** In 2004, San Andreas went bigger again. Three cities, countryside and desert on one single map, set in early-nineties California. You could swim, fly a plane, get a haircut, even put on weight. It became the best-selling game on the PlayStation 2.

**Visual:** Map grows: one city → three cities, then hills and desert fill the gaps. A podium appears with a '#1 on PS2' badge on 'best-selling'.

**On-screen text:** SAN ANDREAS · 2004 · #1 on PS2 · **Source:** G*

### 2005: Hot Coffee

#### 15. The hidden scene · `15-hotcoffee` · 4:07

**Voice-over (29 words):** Then, in 2005, modders found something left on the disc. An unfinished sex mini-game, nicknamed Hot Coffee. The developers had switched it off. They had never actually removed it.

**Visual:** A game disc opens like a vault; a locked folder inside with a coffee-cup icon. A padlock pops open on 'modders'. Keep it symbolic: cup and steam only.

**On-screen text:** HOT COFFEE · 2005 · **Source:** H

#### 16. The fallout · `16-fallout` · 4:20

**Voice-over (38 words):** America's ratings board changed the game's rating to Adults Only. Big retailers pulled it from their shelves. Take-Two reported more than twenty-four million dollars in costs from returns, and settled with the Federal Trade Commission a year later.

**Visual:** Rating badge flips from 'M' to 'AO'. Shelves empty out box by box. Counter runs to $24.5M. A document stamped 'FTC · 2006'.

**On-screen text:** M → AO · $24.5M in returns · **Source:** H

#### 17. The big argument · `17-debate` · 4:37

**Voice-over (37 words):** By now, the series stood at the centre of a much larger argument. Do violent games harm the people who play them? Campaigners and politicians called for bans. Defenders replied that these were satires, rated for adults.

**Visual:** A balance scale. Left pan: megaphone and 'BAN' signs. Right pan: '18+' badge and a theatre mask for satire. The scale keeps swinging, never settling. Neutral framing.

**On-screen text:** HARMFUL? or SATIRE FOR ADULTS? · **Source:** H

### 2008–2013: Breaking records

#### 18. GTA IV · `18-gta4` · 4:54

**Voice-over (34 words):** April 2008. Grand Theft Auto IV returns to Liberty City, with a darker story. Its hero is Niko Bellic, an immigrant from Eastern Europe, chasing an American dream that keeps slipping out of reach.

**On-screen text:** LIBERTY CITY · April 2008 · **Source:** I

### 2013–2026: The long wait

#### 23. Silence and a leak · `23-wait` · 6:22

**Voice-over (35 words):** For the fans, that success had a price: the wait. Year after year, there was no new chapter. Then, in 2022, a hacker leaked dozens of clips from the next game, still in early development.

**Visual:** A calendar flips 2014, 2015 … 2021 with a restless crowd of figures. On '2022', a pipe bursts and video thumbnails (grey placeholders) spill out.

**On-screen text:** 2013 … 2022 · THE LEAK · **Source:** K*

### What GTA means

#### 27. Three readings · `27-meaning` · 7:29

**Voice-over (36 words):** So what is Grand Theft Auto? For its critics, a bad influence. For its fans, a sharp parody of America: its money, its media, its excess. For the industry, the standard everyone else is measured against.

**Visual:** Three panels pop in on 'critics', 'fans', 'industry': warning sign / laughing theatre mask / ruler with other game boxes lined up beneath it. Equal size, equal weight.

**On-screen text:** CRITICS · FANS · INDUSTRY

#### 28. Back to Dundee · `28-end` · 7:46

**Voice-over (33 words):** And all of it leads back to Dundee. To a failing racing game, and to police cars that would not stop ramming the player. Most bugs get fixed. This one built an empire.

**Visual:** Full timeline 1997 → 2026 rewinds at speed back to the Dundee pin and the first top-down scene. The glitching police car is the last thing on screen; fade to channel end card.

**On-screen text:** Most bugs get fixed. This one built an empire. · **Source:** A
